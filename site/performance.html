Three years of ETH funding, earned from the short side

Long spot ETH, short an equal amount of ETH perpetuals on Hyperliquid, and collect the funding that longs pay shorts every hour. This page shows what that position would have earned from May 2023 to September 2026, computed from Hyperliquid's public funding history.

How the data was collected

  1. Pulled every ETH funding payment from the Hyperliquid public API, back to the first ETH record on 12 May 2023.
  2. Aggregated 28,668 payments into 1,219 daily rates. Hyperliquid paid funding every 8 hours in the first weeks of data (until 7 June 2023) and every hour since 8 June 2023.
  3. Took the short's view. A positive rate means longs pay shorts, so it is income for this position. Negative days count as losses.
  4. Annualised without compounding and scaled to the 71.25% of NAV that earns funding at 3× leverage with a 5% liquidity buffer: (1 − 5%) ÷ (1 + 1/3).

Read with these limits

  • Returns are the funding earned on the short leg, before trading fees for entry, exit and rebalancing. We estimate those fees at 0.3–0.5% a year with daily netting and maker execution.
  • The ETH spot–perp basis is assumed flat. Short-term basis moves change mark-to-market, not the funding earned.
  • APR figures are simple averages and do not compound. Funding income was lower in 2025 and 2026 than in 2023–24.
  • Leverage is capped at 3×, so 71% of NAV earns funding instead of 91% in a 10× structure. That lowers the yield but leaves the short with far more margin.
  • Figures are before fund costs (administration, depositary, audit) and trading costs. Returns vary and can be negative. Past funding is not a forecast.
